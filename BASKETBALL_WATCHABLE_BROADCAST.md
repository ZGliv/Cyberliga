# Cyberliga Basketball — Watchable Broadcast Contract

**Status:** isolated off-product showcase

**Promise:** In one autonomous loop, a first-time viewer can understand who Stoopy and Toque are, what each one is
trying to do, how the matchup changes, why the final possession matters, and what to watch next.

## 1. The show, not the demo

The visual-reset possession proved a material and character-rendering direction. It did not prove a product anyone
would voluntarily watch. The completed showcase is a 75–90 second broadcast story with a beginning, escalation,
reversal and consequence. It is not a montage of disconnected dunks, a technical animation reel or a UI dashboard.

The viewer should be able to say, without reading this contract:

- “Stoopy creates space with his body, but his heavy landings cost him a second effort.”
- “Toque is the long, fast answer who can erase a clean look, but traffic can take away the release.”
- “Toronto adjusted to the screen; New York then won the second effort.”

## 2. Required story arc

1. **Cold open.** Establish the High Room, one-score fourth-quarter stakes and the two exact rivals before action.
2. **Statement.** New York runs the Stoop Drop. Screen, pocket catch, late Toque rotation, certified finish.
3. **Truthful replay.** One short replay shows the physical cause, never inventing footage or hiding contact.
4. **Answer.** Toronto creates Toque's Whiteout Release for a certified reply.
5. **Adjustment.** Toronto switches the next screen and forces a real turnover rather than replaying the same beat.
6. **Erasure.** Toque blocks or forces a miss at the rim; score stays unchanged until the outcome is physically clear.
7. **Second effort.** The loose-ball/rebound race pays off Stoopy's authored recovery flaw, then a later legal second
   effort creates the final putback rather than granting an unexplained hero result.
8. **Consequence.** The broadcast shows the final score, the rivalry state and one reason to watch the next meeting.
9. **Reset.** Graphics and audio clear before the deterministic loop returns to the cold open.

Every score change follows its physical scoring contact. A miss, block, turnover or loose ball changes zero points.

## 3. Character identity

### Stoopy — New York Brownstones

- Gift: **MOVING ARCHITECTURE** — a planted screen, roll seal or boxout changes real space.
- Flaw: **HEAVY FOUNDATION** — a hard landing delays the immediate second jump.
- Shape language: broad, low, offset masonry masses; deliberate feet; cyan pigment only as identity.
- Broadcast language: weight, patience, geometry, “the building moved.”

### Toque — Toronto Lake Effect

- Gift: **WHITEOUT RELEASE** — length and timing erase or create a clean airborne window.
- Flaw: **TRAFFIC LOST** — two bodies in the release corridor disrupt the clean timing.
- Shape language: tall narrow taper, long limbs, wind-ribbon accent; pink pigment only as identity.
- Broadcast language: sudden reach, cold timing, “the window disappeared.”

No generic nameplate is allowed to substitute for readable motion. Text names what the viewer has already seen.

## 4. Broadcast grammar

- Live action begins in a stable tactical master containing ball, primary matchup, passing lane and complete rim.
- A push or pan must be motivated by the ball or the matchup. No orbiting showroom camera.
- Do not cut on screen contact, catch, shot/block contact, rim crossing or landing.
- A replay may use one different angle and controlled slow motion. It carries `REPLAY` and recorded-footage truth.
- Dead-ball/context cards may appear only after the outcome. During live play show score, clock and possession only.
- At least 1.5 seconds of visual breathing room separates major outcomes.
- The final consequence shot keeps both rivals in one picture; no isolated hero worship.

## 5. Graphics package

- One compact scorebug: club marks, score, fourth-quarter clock, shot clock and possession.
- One top-right mode bug: `LIVE`, `REPLAY`, `DEAD BALL` or `FINAL`; never two modes at once.
- Cold-open matchup card: exact names, gifts and one-score stakes; clears before the ball is live.
- Short factual lower thirds for certified outcomes only. No fake odds, money, authority or invented popularity.
- One optional commentator line during dead time; maximum 11 words and never required to understand the play.
- A single sound control may unlock/mute arena audio. It is not a gameplay, pause, restart or outcome control.
- Desktop, 390×844 and native 1080×1920 receive independently composed cameras and safe-zone layouts.

## 6. Procedural sound contract

Sound is causal, sparse and synchronized to the rendered event. Local Web Audio synthesis is allowed; external
assets and network audio are not required.

- **Room bus:** restrained High Room air and crowd bed; never a constant broadband hiss.
- **Court bus:** spatially biased dribbles, pass/catch, separate shoe squeaks and body/screen contact.
- **Basket bus:** distinct rim, net, block and floor contacts.
- **Crowd bus:** anticipation follows advantage; reaction begins after the physical result, with home/away character.
- **Broadcast bus:** cold-open sting, replay transition, certified-score punctuation, final horn.
- Master output is compressed and bounded. A single cue cannot exceed the score stinger or cause clipping.
- User gesture unlock is honest. Before unlock, state reports `locked`; no phantom cue is marked played.
- Seeking, reset and loop changes cannot duplicate a cue or play an event from the wrong possession.

## 7. Atmosphere and arena life

- Three crowd-depth bands remain visually distinct. Response moves from rim-side/courtside to the upper room.
- Benches, officials and inactive players react later and more quietly than the primary bodies.
- The building may breathe through hard-light changes, scoreboard practicals and restrained fabric motion.
- No bloom fog, confetti spam, particles used as impact, pulsing neon or cloned dot-crowd wallpaper.
- The court and athletes remain the brightest readable hierarchy at every live-action frame.

## 8. Determinism and truth

- Director, possessions, score, camera, graphics and cue plan are deterministic and deeply frozen.
- The loop contains five physically distinct outcomes: make, made reply, turnover, block/miss and rebound/putback.
- All ten actors remain exact canonical New York or Toronto athletes; possession sides switch truthfully.
- `render_game_to_text` exposes show segment, possession, outcome, score/clock, camera, audio status and visible graphics.
- No `Math.random`, `Date`, storage, network or audience/merch/wager field can alter gameplay or results.
- Capture mode can seek to any millisecond without changing the official story.

## 9. Acceptance gate

The showcase does not replace public Basketball until all of the following pass on exact committed bytes:

- The uninterrupted show completes and loops three times with identical state digests and no void/reset jump.
- Every required story beat and all five outcome kinds occur in physical and certification order.
- Desktop, 390×844 and 1080×1920 contact sheets contain the ball, principals and relevant rim at every contact.
- Live graphics never cover a principal, ball, rim or contact; dead-ball graphics remain inside their reserved zone.
- Browser console, page, request and HTTP errors are zero; all exposed state and scene transforms are finite.
- Horizontal overflow is zero; touch targets for Globe and Sound are at least 44 px when shown.
- Audio cue tests prove causal order, once-per-event playback, honest lock/mute state and reset/seek safety.
- Reference desktop p95 frame time is at most 20 ms and mobile p95 at most 33.3 ms, with device/browser recorded.
- A ten-person blind comparison reaches at least 8/10 for play comprehension, camera clarity and preferred replay;
  at least 6/10 remember both Stoopy and Toque unaided and at least 5/10 want to watch the next possession.

Passing automated and browser checks proves implementation integrity. The blind comparison proves watchability.
