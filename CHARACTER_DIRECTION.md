# CYBERLIGA Cast Reset

The rejected chibi/humanoid portrait set is not the Cyberliga cast. Blobs remain the
public match bodies while a replacement visual language is explored off-product.

## Viral character gate

A character only ships when it passes all six tests:

1. Recognizable as a black silhouette at 48 px.
2. Built around a motion signature that can become a reaction GIF or meme.
3. Uses a different body family, center of gravity, and rhythm from every other star.
4. Reads as a sports competitor without generic cyberpunk armor or robot parts.
5. Can be reconstructed as a small rig with a few expressive masses, not a fragile illustration.
6. Remains the same identity in the match, portraits, clips, posters, and physical merchandise.

## First GPT Image exploration prompt

Use case: stylized-concept

Asset type: off-product character art-direction lineup for an original viral sports universe

Primary request: six completely different fictional athletic beings that could become the
recognizable stars of football, basketball, hockey, baseball, and combat leagues. Each must
have a radically different silhouette, body construction, center of gravity, and emotional
energy. Explore a squat rolling powerhouse, a tall ribbon-limbed aerial player, a compact
spring-loaded speedster, a broad soft goalkeeper form, a crooked long-armed enforcer, and
one unexpected non-humanoid athlete.

Style/medium: tactile stop-motion maquettes made from dyed foam, clay, fabric, rubber, and
painted wood; handmade imperfections; bold editorial sports photography; strange, physical,
funny, intimidating, and instantly memorable rather than cute or sleek.

Composition/framing: one full-body lineup on a neutral dark studio cyclorama, all six bodies
fully visible with generous separation, straight-on three-quarter poses, readable at thumbnail
size, no labels.

Constraints: original designs; simple riggable masses; real sports-wear cues without logos;
no repeated head/body template; no text; no watermark.

Avoid: robots, generic cyberpunk, armor, Funko/chibi proportions, photoreal humans, mascot
costumes, superheroes, existing IP, identical faces, glossy concept-art sameness.
